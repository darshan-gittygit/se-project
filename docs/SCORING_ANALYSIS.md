## Win share over every possible trait vector

All 1,953,125 vectors with each of the 9 traits in 1-5, scored by the engine's own formula and tie-break (`python -m scripts.win_share`).

| V-Model | 10.0% | 53.7 | 7.5 | 100.0 | 35.9 |
| Waterfall | 8.5% | 54.5 | 7.5 | 100.0 | 35.9 |

A project rated 3 on every trait scores: Incremental 83.2, Spiral 72.7, Agile 65.5, V-Model 57.5, Waterfall 57.5.

Reading: no model gets a bonus (each scores exactly 100 on its own profile, and the formula is the same for all), but the profiles are not equally central. Incremental's profile sits nearest the middle of the scale, so no project can be far from it (largest possible weighted distance 25.9, against 35.9 for Waterfall), and it wins the largest share of the uniform space. Real projects are not uniformly spread over that space, so this is a property of the profile geometry to be aware of, not a measured recommendation rate. The profiles and weights were deliberately NOT adjusted to equalise these shares: that would trade documented engineering judgement for an arbitrary statistical target.

### Every flip

- **social media**: none
- **payroll**: rating criticality -1 -> Waterfall; rating verification_validation -1 -> Waterfall
- **railway**: none

Reading: weights and profiles are expert judgement, so the question is whether the recommendations depend on the exact numbers chosen. Flips in A-C mean that winner rests on a specific weight or profile value; flips in D show which single rating error by the LLM would change the answer. Close calls (small margins) are where flips concentrate, which is why the UI labels results within 5 points as close calls.
