Generated by `python -m scripts.sensitivity --write`. Offline and deterministic.

## Win share over the trait-vector space

A seeded uniform random sample of 1,000,000 vectors (each of the 15 traits in 1-5; standard error at most 0.05 points), scored by the engine's own formula and tie-break (`python -m scripts.win_share`). Lowest and highest possible scores are exact.

| Model | Wins (share of all vectors) | Mean score | Lowest possible | Highest | Largest possible weighted distance from its profile |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Incremental | 31.1% | 66.4 | 35.5 | 100.0 | 37.9 |
| Spiral | 18.4% | 61.2 | 24.5 | 100.0 | 44.4 |
| Agile-V | 16.6% | 64.2 | 27.9 | 100.0 | 42.4 |
| DevSecOps | 11.8% | 62.5 | 23.3 | 100.0 | 45.1 |
| Agile | 9.1% | 60.4 | 20.1 | 100.0 | 47.0 |
| Waterfall | 7.6% | 57.5 | 14.5 | 100.0 | 50.3 |
| V-Model | 5.4% | 56.8 | 14.1 | 100.0 | 50.5 |

A project rated 3 on every trait scores: Incremental 85.5, Agile-V 77.9, Spiral 74.5, DevSecOps 73.3, Agile 70.1, Waterfall 64.5, V-Model 64.1.

Reading: no model gets a bonus (each scores exactly 100 on its own profile, and the formula is the same for all), but the profiles are not equally central. Incremental's profile sits nearest the middle of the scale, so no project can be far from it (largest possible weighted distance 37.9, against 50.5 for V-Model), and it wins the largest share of the uniform space. Real projects are not uniformly spread over that space, so this is a property of the profile geometry to be aware of, not a measured recommendation rate. The profiles and weights were deliberately NOT adjusted to equalise these shares: that would trade documented engineering judgement for an arbitrary statistical target.

### Every flip

- **social media**: none
- **payroll**: rating complexity -1 -> Waterfall; rating criticality -1 -> Waterfall; rating verification_validation -1 -> Waterfall
- **railway**: none
- **food delivery**: DevSecOps risk_uncertainty 2->1 -> Incremental; DevSecOps risk_uncertainty 2->3 -> Incremental; rating complexity -1 -> Incremental; rating verification_validation -1 -> Incremental; rating stakeholder_feedback -1 -> Incremental; rating security_risk -1 -> Incremental; rating legacy_dependence +1 -> Incremental; rating continuous_delivery -1 -> Incremental; rating stakeholder_availability -1 -> Incremental

Reading: weights and profiles are expert judgement, so the question is whether the recommendations depend on the exact numbers chosen. Flips in A-C mean that winner rests on a specific weight or profile value; flips in D show which single rating error by the LLM would change the answer. Close calls (small margins) are where flips concentrate, which is why the UI labels results within 5 points as close calls.
