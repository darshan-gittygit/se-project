Data Protection and Privacy Principles

Modern data protection frameworks (such as India's Digital Personal Data Protection
Act and comparable frameworks elsewhere) are generally built around a common set of
principles: collect only the personal data actually needed for the stated purpose
(data minimization), obtain meaningful consent before collecting or processing
personal data, and give individuals the ability to access, correct, or request
deletion of their data.

Systems that process personal data - names, contact details, financial information,
health records, government identity numbers - are typically expected to: state
clearly what data is being collected and why, avoid using the data for purposes
beyond what was disclosed, protect the data with reasonable technical safeguards
(encryption in transit and at rest, access controls), and notify affected
individuals and, in many frameworks, a regulator, in the event of a data breach.

Sensitive personal data - health information, financial account details, biometric
data, government ID numbers - usually warrants a higher standard of protection than
general personal data, including stricter access controls and, in many frameworks,
explicit rather than implied consent.

A well-designed software system handling personal data should generally include:
a documented lawful basis or consent step before data collection, encryption of
data at rest and in transit, role-based access control so only authorized staff or
systems can view sensitive fields, and a data retention policy rather than
indefinite storage. Systems that collect more personal data than necessary for
their stated purpose, or that lack basic encryption and access controls, are
generally considered to carry elevated compliance and privacy risk.
