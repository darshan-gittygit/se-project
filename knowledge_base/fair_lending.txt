Fair Lending and Responsible Credit Assessment Principles

Credit and lending systems are generally expected to base approval and pricing
decisions on objective, disclosed criteria rather than protected characteristics
such as religion, caste, gender, or ethnicity. Regulators in most jurisdictions,
including India's fair practices guidance for banks and NBFCs, expect lenders to
be able to explain the basis for a credit decision if challenged - a system that
produces a rejection with no explainable reasoning is generally considered a
compliance and fairness risk, not just a UX shortcoming.

Responsible lending principles typically require that a system assess a
borrower's actual repayment capacity (income, existing obligations, credit
history) rather than approving credit based on incomplete information, and that
loan terms, interest rates, and fees be disclosed clearly before the borrower
commits.

Automated or algorithmic credit-scoring components carry additional expectations:
the scoring logic should be periodically reviewed for disparate impact on
protected groups, and borrowers who are declined should generally receive a
reason for the decision, not just a rejection notice.

A digital lending system should typically include: a documented, explainable
credit-scoring methodology, disclosure of all loan terms before commitment, a
process for borrowers to request the reason for a declined application, and
periodic review of automated decisions for fairness. Systems using an
unexplainable "black box" credit decision with no borrower recourse would
generally be considered high-risk under fair lending expectations.
