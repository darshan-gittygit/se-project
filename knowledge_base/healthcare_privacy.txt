Healthcare Data Privacy Principles

Systems that handle patient health information - medical records, diagnoses,
prescriptions, appointment or consultation data - are generally expected to
follow a stricter privacy standard than typical personal data, commonly
summarized under frameworks like HIPAA (in the US) or equivalent health-data
provisions within broader data protection laws elsewhere.

Core expectations typically include: restricting access to patient records to
authorized clinical staff on a need-to-know basis, maintaining an audit trail of
who accessed a patient's record and when, encrypting health data both at rest and
in transit, and obtaining explicit patient consent before sharing health data
with third parties (insurers, other providers, researchers).

Telehealth and remote-consultation systems carry additional expectations: video
or audio consultation streams should be encrypted end-to-end where feasible,
session recordings (if any) should be subject to the same access controls as
written records, and patients should be clearly informed about how their
consultation data is stored and for how long.

A system handling patient health data should typically include: role-based access
control limited to relevant clinical staff, encryption of records and any
video/audio streams, an access audit log, and an explicit consent step before
data is shared externally. Systems that allow broad, unrestricted staff access to
patient records or store consultation data without encryption would generally be
considered high-risk from a healthcare privacy standpoint.
