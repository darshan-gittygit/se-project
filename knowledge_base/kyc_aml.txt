Know Your Customer (KYC) and Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Principles

Financial institutions offering account opening, lending, or payment services are
generally expected to verify the identity of a customer before establishing a
business relationship. This typically involves collecting and validating official
identity documents, proof of address, and, for certain products, proof of income
or financial standing.

Systems that onboard customers for loans or other credit products should capture
enough identity and background information to support a risk-based assessment -
higher-risk customers or transaction patterns typically warrant deeper verification
("enhanced due diligence"), while lower-risk, well-documented customers may go
through a simpler process.

AML principles require institutions to monitor transactions for patterns that could
indicate money laundering or fraud - for example, unusually large transactions,
rapid movement of funds, or structuring transactions to avoid reporting thresholds.
Systems handling financial transactions are generally expected to log transaction
details in a way that supports later audit and investigation, and to flag
suspicious patterns for human review rather than silently allowing them.

A digital loan origination or account-opening system should therefore typically
include: identity document capture and verification, address verification, a
risk-scoring step to classify customer risk level, and an audit trail of
verification decisions. Systems that skip identity verification entirely, or that
do not retain verification records, would generally be considered non-compliant
with standard KYC/AML expectations in most jurisdictions, including India's
regulatory environment for banks and NBFCs.
