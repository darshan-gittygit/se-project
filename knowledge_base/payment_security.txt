Payment and Cardholder Data Security Principles

Systems that store, process, or transmit payment card information are generally
expected to follow industry security standards commonly summarized as: never store
full card verification codes after transaction authorization, encrypt cardholder
data both in transit and at rest, restrict access to payment data on a
strict need-to-know basis, and maintain detailed logs of who accessed payment data
and when.

Best practice is to avoid storing raw card numbers wherever possible - using
tokenization (replacing the card number with a non-sensitive token) or delegating
card storage entirely to a certified third-party payment processor is generally
preferred over building in-house card storage.

Systems should also implement transaction-level safeguards: enforcing secure
communication protocols (such as TLS) for any payment-related network traffic,
rate-limiting or flagging repeated failed payment attempts (a common fraud
indicator), and providing users with a clear transaction receipt or confirmation
record.

A system that directly handles payments should typically include: encrypted
transmission of payment details, tokenization or delegation to a compliant payment
processor rather than raw card storage, fraud-pattern monitoring on transactions,
and a transaction audit log. Systems that store raw, unencrypted card numbers or
lack any fraud monitoring would generally be considered high-risk from a payment
security standpoint.
